P4 — The two channels on their shared floodplain (presentation figure)

Composite cross-section of the floodplain corridor at matched distance from
the anchor: the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek cut into the one plain they
share, with the ~2.6 km of corridor between them collapsed to an axis break.
Both drawn from the measured medians of the 63-arc analysis, against the
same floodplain-corridor reference used throughout: the Kanektok's median
water surface sits 1.50 m below the plain (SWOT KaRIn, median stage), the
Uyak's 0.49 m below — the Uyak is the low slough of the floodplain, nearly
at grade (above the corridor on ~30% of arcs), while the Kanektok is set a
full metre deeper. The independent pass-paired comparison gives the same
answer: the Uyak's water surface rides +0.96 m above the Kanektok's at
matched radius, on 100% of passes (2,495 pass–radius pairs, 49 passes).
Neither channel is perched above the corridor it would spill into, and the
Kanektok is already the topographic low — water leaving it would have to
end up higher.

Channel widths are drawn to relative scale (Kanektok ~50 m, Uyak ~30 m).
The Kanektok bed hangs one measured ADCP depth (1.30 m, per-arc median)
below its water line; the Uyak bed is dashed because its depth (1.08 m)
was measured only at the mouth reach (radius 31–33 km), the one reach the
boat survey covered on both rivers. Same true vertical scale as figures
P1–P3; the horizontal axis is compressed and the inter-channel corridor
omitted, so bank slopes appear far steeper than the gentle grades seen on
the ground.

Data: SWOT L2 HR PIXC water surface elevations / ArcticDEM digital surface
model / boat ADCP survey; all on the EGM2008 datum. All "below the plain"
statements are stage statements quoted at median stage: across the observed
range the Kanektok spans −1.75 m (low water) to −1.01 m (high), the Uyak
−0.77 m to −0.14 m.
