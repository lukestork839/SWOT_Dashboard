P7b — The same scene with the SWOT node cloud (presentation figure)

The partner panel to P7: identical extent, figure size, basemap zoom, scale bar
and north arrow, with every SWOT node in frame drawn on top at the same 0.09
alpha used for the node clouds in Figures 1 and 10. Nothing else changes
between the two, so shown side by side the only difference is the data.
Presented left (imagery) to right (imagery plus data), the pair answers
"where does the measurement actually come from?" in one gesture.

Dots are drawn slightly larger here than in the thesis maps (s=4.5, against 1.8
in Figure 1 and 2.6 in Figure 10) because each SWOT pixel covers far more
screen area at this zoom; at the smaller size the sampling lattice itself
becomes visible and reads as noise rather than as data. The opacity is
unchanged, so density still means density: darker areas are areas observed on
more passes.

The panel carries no legend: it is meant to be shown beside the overlay-free
one, where the imagery is the subject and a keyed box in the corner would both
cover it and pull the eye off it. Red is the Kanektok River, blue is Uyak
Creek — the same colours used throughout the series — named on the slide or in
the talk rather than in the frame.

Three things are worth pointing at while the slide is up.

The clouds do trace real water. Both follow the braid threads visible in the
left panel closely, including the minor anabranches, which is the reassuring
half of the comparison — the pixel classification is finding channels, not
painting the floodplain at random.

The Uyak's cloud is nonetheless far broader than the Uyak. In the left panel
its channel is a single thread a few image pixels wide; on the right it is a
band comparable in width to the Kanektok's. This is the corridor artifact
described in P7's caption, made visible rather than asserted: the analysis
polygon is fitted around the braid plain the pixels are drawn from, not around
the channel.

The straight diagonal seam where blue gives way to red is a polygon boundary,
not a river feature. The two analysis polygons overlap along the shared reach
upstream of the split (~4% of the smaller polygon's area, and both contain the
bifurcation point itself); Uyak Creek is drawn last here, as in every other
figure in the series, so that the narrower river is never buried, and in the
overlap its blue therefore covers the Kanektok's red. The consequence to name
out loud is that the wide bright channel entering from the east — the shared
reach above the bifurcation — appears blue, which should not be read as the
Uyak being that wide there.

Basemap: Esri World Imagery, fetched at zoom 15; the scale bar is corrected for
the latitude distortion of the Web Mercator projection. Nodes are the
open-water-classified archive, unthinned within the frame.
