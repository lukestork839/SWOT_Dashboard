P6 — The bifurcation virtual gauge (presentation figure)

Local hydraulic gradient and water surface at a virtual gauge on each river,
1 km along-channel downstream of the bifurcation (analysis:
bifurcation_gauge.py; results in
temporal_results/bifurcation_gauge_per_pass.parquet).

Two rulers are used deliberately. GRADIENT is fitted on the project-wide
radial dist_km axis over the fixed 3.5–7.5 km window: pixel water-surface
elevations are binned to 0.5 km nodes (9 nodes) and a Theil–Sen line through
the node medians gives the local gradient. The window sits entirely below the
split (2.49 km) and entirely clear of the zone where the two reach polygons
overlap, so no shared water enters either fit. Radial distance compresses a
curving channel, so these gradients run ~1.47× steeper than true along-channel
water-surface slopes; the inflation is near-identical for the two rivers on
this window (1.473× vs 1.459×, a 0.9% mismatch), which is why the radial axis
is kept — it is the fairest ruler for a BETWEEN-river comparison.

STAGE is measured at a gauge sited by along-channel distance: the point 1 km
downstream of the bifurcation on each river's official centerline, with stage
the observed median WSE of that river's pixels within 300 m of it. The two
gauges are 740 m apart and each disc draws on one reach mask only — zero
pixels from the other river. Along-channel siting is required: a radial ring
1 km from the split would cross the Kanektok at 0.97 channel-km but the Uyak
at 1.18, and at ~1.7 m of drop per channel-km that 0.21 km mismatch is worth
~0.35 m of water surface, enough to reverse the sign of the stage comparison.
A pass qualifies only when all 9 nodes are present with at least 20 pixels
each and the gauge disc holds at least 20 pixels (95 Kanektok, 89 Uyak passes).

(a) Each river's local gradient against its own gauge stage, with dotted
per-river median lines: Kanektok median 261.0 cm/km [IQR 259.2–262.7], Uyak
Creek 229.0 [225.4–233.4]. The two clouds never mix: pairing the passes, the
Kanektok is steeper on 100% of the 89 paired passes, median advantage
+32.4 cm/km [IQR +28.9 to +35.3]. The advantage does not merely survive
rising water — it GROWS with it, by +13.8 cm/km per metre of stage
(Kendall tau = +0.346, p < 0.0001; low-stage half +29.5 cm/km, high-stage
half +33.7). The Uyak's own gradient falls faster with stage than the
Kanektok's (−12.9 vs −4.4 cm/km per m), which is what widens the gap: the
secondary channel is the more backwater-sensitive of the two. The trend is
not an artifact of the record — stage is uncorrelated with year (tau = −0.02)
and the trend reproduces within 2024 (p = 0.012) and 2025 (p = 0.002).
Avulsions initiate at high flow; at these gauges the occupied channel's local
gradient advantage is largest exactly there.

(b) The two water surfaces below the split, median across passes with the
interquartile band. They leave the bifurcation together — 0.06 m apart at
0.75 km, the first station where the two 300 m discs stop sharing pixels —
and separate downstream as the steeper Kanektok outruns the Uyak, reaching
0.53 m by 3 km. The bumps are real local slope structure (braid nodes and
riffles), not sampling noise; the overall separation rate, ~21 cm/km in
along-channel terms, independently reproduces the ~32 cm/km radial advantage
in (a) once the 1.47× radial inflation is removed.

Gauge-siting sensitivity: moving both gauges to 1.25 km past the split
(channels 982 m apart) leaves the paired result unchanged (+32.4 cm/km, 100%
of 88 passes) and the stage gap at −0.007 m, but the per-pass correlation
between the two gauge stages falls from 0.806 to 0.652 — 1 km is the better
sited pair, and the headline numbers do not depend on the choice.

Because the fitting window is fixed, these metrics are immune to the
coverage-truncation artifact that displaces two 2023 Uyak passes in the
whole-reach time series (Figures 16–18): every gated pass is fitted over the
same 4 km.

Data: SWOT L2 HR PIXC water-surface elevations, frozen master archive
(May–October 2023–2026). Stage sampling caveat: SWOT's 21-day repeat rarely
catches flood peaks, so "every observed stage" spans the sampled ~1 m range,
not the full flood envelope.
