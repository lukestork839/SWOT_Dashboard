P2 — Superelevation comparison, highest-beta arc (presentation figure)

Companion to P1: instead of the median geometry, panel (b) shows the single
arc where the Kanektok comes closest to the superelevated configuration.
(a) The avulsion-ready superelevated channel, schematic after Gearon et al.
(2024): bed aggraded to floodplain grade, beta = H_AR/H_M ~ 1, water above
the plain. (b) The 25.0 km arc — the beta maximum of the 63 depth-valid arcs
(values from the tracked arcB_channels.parquet; canonical ridge-claim audit).
A real alluvial ridge exists here: the crest stands H_AR = +2.34 m above the
floodplain reference, against a crest-to-bed depth H_M = 4.72 m, giving
beta = 0.495 — the highest on the reach; no arc reaches 0.5. Even at this
maximum, the median water surface (SWOT KaRIn) sits 1.06 m BELOW the
floodplain reference: the ridge is tall because the channel is deep, not
because the water rides high. The cross-section is drawn as the arc
transects actually present: high ground flanks both sides of the channel —
a tall bank directly at the channel (the crest pick, the highest DSM point
within +/-150 m, plausibly including riparian canopy) and a second ridge
set back behind a low bench on the opposite side — the configuration a
visitor to the river would recognize. Both panels share the same true
vertical scale; the horizontal
axis is compressed, so bank slopes appear far steeper than the gentle grades
seen on the ground. The bed is drawn one H_M below the crest, so the drawn
water depth (1.32 m) differs slightly from the arc's ADCP depth (1.28 m).

Data: SWOT L2 HR PIXC water surface elevations / ArcticDEM digital surface
model / boat ADCP survey. Panel (a) is a conceptual schematic, not data.
The DSM canopy caveat applies to the crest pick: vegetation bias inflates
crests, so the positive H_AR is an upper bound and beta = 0.495 is
conservative for the no-superelevation conclusion.
