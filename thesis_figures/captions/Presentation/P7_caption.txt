P7a — The two channels in raw satellite imagery (presentation figure)

Esri World Imagery of the bifurcation and the first few kilometres of each
channel, with no analysis overlays: scale bar and north arrow only. The frame
is ~3 km across and centred on the split (59.8289 N, 161.3778 W), which sits
just left of centre where the narrow channel departs northwest from the broad
one.

The Kanektok River leaves the split as the wide, bright, multi-thread braid
that fills the lower half of the frame, with exposed gravel bars between
threads. Uyak Creek leaves it as the single dark thread running northwest
across the upper left — narrow enough that it is only a few image pixels wide
and, at the extent used for Figure 10, not resolvable at all.

This figure exists because the analysis maps cannot make that point. In
Figure 10 each river is drawn as its analysis polygon — a corridor fitted
around the whole braid plain, not around the channel — so the Uyak's mask
reads as wide as the Kanektok's. That is correct for its purpose (it is the
region SWOT pixels are drawn from) and misleading as a picture of channel
size. Stripping the overlays away removes the question-begging.

Two caveats belong with any width read off this image. First, the Esri mosaic
carries an acquisition seam running diagonally across the scene: the northern
half — the Uyak's floodplain — is from a paler, drier-looking pass than the
southern half, so part of the apparent tonal contrast is the imagery rather
than the rivers. Second, a single scene is a single unknown stage, so the
width contrast shown is one snapshot and not a stage-averaged measurement.
The quantitative comparison between the two channels is carried by the gauge
and gradient figures, not by this one; this figure is qualitative context.
