P1 — Superelevation comparison (presentation figure)

What an avulsion-ready channel looks like versus what was measured. (a) A
superelevated channel, drawn schematically after Gearon et al. (2024) with
their notation: the alluvial ridge stands H_AR above the floodplain, the
channel depth H_M is measured crest to bed, and with the bed aggraded to
floodplain grade the superelevation ratio beta = H_AR/H_M approaches 1 — the
water surface rides above the plain it would spill into. (b) The Kanektok
River's measured median cross-section geometry (64 DEM arcs): the
near-channel crest sits +0.14 m above the floodplain reference (H_AR), the
median water surface sits 1.50 m below it (SWOT KaRIn, median stage), and the
bed hangs one measured ADCP depth (1.30 m) below the water line, giving
H_M = 2.86 m and beta = 0.06; on 38% of arcs the crest sits at or below the
floodplain (beta <= 0). Both panels share the same true vertical scale; the
horizontal axis is compressed (a schematic ~hundreds of metres), so bank
slopes appear far steeper than the gentle grades seen on the ground. The
individual medians are taken over
slightly different arc populations, so the drawn bed (water - depth) and the
median H_M agree approximately rather than exactly.

Data: SWOT L2 HR PIXC water surface elevations / ArcticDEM digital surface
model / boat ADCP survey. Panel (a) is a conceptual schematic, not data.
