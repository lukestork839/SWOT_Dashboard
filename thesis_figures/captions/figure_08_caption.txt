Figure 8. Interval (local) hydraulic gradient profiles of the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek. Slopes were computed as the numerical derivative of the 100 m median water-surface-elevation bins after a 2 km one-dimensional Gaussian smoothing window, and are plotted as absolute steepness (cm/km) against downriver distance from the anchor point. In both channels the local gradient decreases smoothly downstream, from roughly 230–250 cm/km in the upper reach near the anchor to about 40 cm/km at the mouth; this monotonic decline reflects the expected concave-up longitudinal profile rather than any channel instability. Critically, the decline is gradual, with no abrupt knickpoints or localized slope spikes, and the two profiles track one another closely and interweave along their length rather than one channel remaining persistently steeper. The reach-wide characteristic gradients themselves are near-identical between the two channels (Section 5.2). The absence of concentrated gradient anomalies indicates that neither channel develops the localized steepening that would promote channel aggradation or instability.
