Figure 8. Interval (local) hydraulic gradient profiles of the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek. Slopes were computed as the numerical derivative of the 100 m median water-surface-elevation bins after a one-dimensional Gaussian smoothing with a 2 km standard deviation (σ = 2 km, corresponding to an effective resolution of ~4.7 km full-width-at-half-maximum), and are plotted as absolute steepness (cm/km) against downriver distance from the anchor point. At this smoothing scale the local gradient in both channels decreases smoothly downstream, from roughly 230–250 cm/km in the upper reach near the anchor to about 40 cm/km at the mouth; this monotonic decline reflects the expected concave-up longitudinal profile rather than any channel instability. The decline is gradual, with no abrupt knickpoints, and the two profiles track one another closely and interweave along their length rather than one channel remaining persistently steeper; the reach-wide characteristic gradients themselves are near-identical between the two channels (Section 5.2). The 2 km smoothing deliberately suppresses structure below the backwater scale, however, so this profile should be read as the reach-scale envelope; the backwater-scale (~0.5 km) gradient, including the pronounced steepening of both channels near the bifurcation, is resolved in Figure 9. The absence of concentrated gradient anomalies at reach scale indicates that neither channel develops the sustained localized steepening that would promote channel aggradation or instability.
