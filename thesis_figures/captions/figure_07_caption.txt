Figure 7. Detrended relative elevation profiles of the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek. Residuals were obtained by subtracting a single second-order polynomial fit to both rivers pooled — the shared regional gradient — from the measured water-surface elevations, so the flat zero-line represents that common baseline and each river's residual expresses its structural offset from it. Solid lines are median residuals in 0.5 km distance bins and shaded bands span the 25th–75th percentile across passes; localized contamination was excluded per river using a residual-domain modified Z-score filter (|Z| > 3.5). Dashed horizontal lines mark each river's reach-wide median residual. The Uyak Creek surface sits above the shared gradient (median +0.52 m) while the Kanektok sits below it (median −0.45 m), a structural separation of roughly one metre that is most pronounced through the lower-middle reach (approximately 5–18 km) and narrows near the anchor point and the mouth. This relative arrangement — the Kanektok occupying a topographic low rather than a superelevated position with respect to its neighbour — is consistent with a geomorphically stable channel.
