Figure 1. Study area and spatial-normalization scheme: the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek near Quinhagak, southwestern Alaska (inset), two adjacent distributary channels that split at a common bifurcation and flow west to the Bering Sea. The base image is Esri World Imagery. Each channel's analysis extent is drawn as a lightly shaded polygon (Kanektok River in red, Uyak Creek in blue), and the cloud of Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) nodes contributing to the analysis is overplotted at low opacity, so more heavily sampled reaches read as darker and the point density traces the braided active channel within each polygon. All along-stream distances used throughout the thesis are measured radially (Haversine) from a single anchor point (white circle, 0 km) on the shared upstream channel, roughly 2.5 km upriver of the bifurcation (white star); this common origin places both channels on one downstream distance axis and is the basis for the spatial normalization applied in Figures 4-8. White triangles mark the 15 km virtual-gauge location on each channel, the fixed stage-reference point used to test stage-invariance in Figure 3. Edge ticks are labelled in geographic coordinates, the scale bar is corrected for the latitude distortion of the Web Mercator projection, and north is toward the top of the map. The inset locates the study area within Alaska as a point, the mapped extent being too small to represent as a box at that scale.
