Figure 3. Temporal stability and stage-invariance of the reach hydraulic gradient for the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek, from the one-time temporal analysis of all full-coverage, open-water passes (2023–2026). (a) Water surface elevation at a fixed virtual gauge 15 km downriver (a stage proxy) and (b) the robust Theil–Sen hydraulic gradient, both plotted against date; each point is one satellite pass. Winter months (December–March), when the rivers freeze and no clean open-water measurement is possible, are shaded, and the landfall of Typhoon Halong is marked (dashed line). Community/hunter reports indicate Uyak Creek was traversable by boat during the 2025 open-water season but not in 2026; notably, the 15 km stage proxy in panel (a) is comparable between the two years, indicating that this reported change in navigability is not explained by reach-scale water level alone (see Discussion). The gradient holds essentially constant across the record (~195 cm/km for the Kanektok, ~192 cm/km for Uyak): seasonal (spring vs. late-summer) and interannual (2024 vs. 2025) changes are under ~1 cm/km against this ~195 cm/km baseline, while the water level varies by only ~0.2–0.5 m, and neither series shows a step change across the typhoon. (c) The hydraulic gradient plotted against the 15 km stage proxy; the flat point bands and level median lines (dotted) show the gradient is independent of water level (stage-invariant), which justifies pooling passes from different seasons and years when characterizing each river's gradient (Figures 4–8). One spring-breakup pass (2025-04-17) flagged as ice-contaminated is excluded. The pre/post-typhoon comparison is interim: post-storm late-summer observations will not be available until July–August 2026, so the typhoon result should be treated as preliminary.
