Figure __. Bifurcation and virtual-gauge detail. Zoomed companion to the study-area map, covering the first ~8 km below the anchor point (0 km, white circle). The white star marks the bifurcation, ~2.5 km downriver of the anchor, where the shared channel splits; the white diamonds mark the two bifurcation virtual gauges, one per channel, each sited 1 km along-channel downstream of the split on that river's official centerline (drawn in the river's colour). The channels are 740 m apart at the gauges, and each gauge's 300 m sampling disc draws on one channel's analysis polygon only. Each channel's analysis polygon is drawn as in the study-area map (Kanektok River in red, Uyak Creek in blue) with the contributing SWOT nodes overplotted at low opacity, so the braided channel threads read directly from sampling density. Note that the two polygons deliberately overlap upstream of and just below the split, where the braid plain is shared and water can enter either channel; the gauges are sited past that zone. The gauges anchor the local stage–gradient analysis: at each, stage is the observed median water-surface elevation within the gauge disc, while the local hydraulic gradient is fitted over the fixed 3.5–7.5 km window on the radial distance axis. Basemap: Esri World Imagery; the scale bar is corrected for the latitude distortion of the Web Mercator projection. Data: SWOT node positions, all analyzed passes.
