Figure 6. Localized water-surface elevation difference between the Kanektok River and the adjacent Uyak Creek (Kanektok minus Uyak), computed per SWOT pass in 100 m distance bins and aggregated across all open-water passes. The bold line is the median difference and the grey band is the 25th–75th percentile across passes, showing that the difference is highly consistent and therefore stable across river stage. The dashed line marks equal water-surface elevation between the two channels; blue shading indicates where the Kanektok sits below Uyak (sub-elevation) and red where it sits above. The Kanektok is near-continuously sub-elevated along its entire course, reaching a maximum deficit of approximately −2.2 m near 9 km downriver. This persistent sub-elevation — the Kanektok occupying a topographic low relative to its neighbour rather than a superelevated ridge — is consistent with a geomorphically stable channel that is not predisposed to avulse toward Uyak Creek.
