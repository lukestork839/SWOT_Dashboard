Figure 4. Distribution of the robust reference hydraulic gradient across individual satellite passes. Each point is the reach-wide gradient of one full-coverage, open-water pass, fit with the Theil–Sen estimator on 1 km along-stream nodes (87 passes for the Kanektok River, 47 for Uyak Creek; a subset of the 121 open-water overpasses retained after full-coverage gating). The bold line marks the median and the shaded band the interquartile range. Both channels share a near-identical baseline gradient, but the Kanektok is exceptionally consistent (median 195.5 cm/km, interquartile range ~1 cm/km) whereas Uyak Creek is slightly more variable (median 192.3 cm/km, interquartile range ~3 cm/km). The two distributions are statistically distinguishable (Mann–Whitney U, p < 0.001), though the median offset is small (~3 cm/km, 1.6%). One spring-breakup pass (2025-04-17), anomalously steep on both channels simultaneously, was flagged as ice-contaminated and excluded; a small number of higher-gradient open-water passes lie beyond the plotted range and are noted on the figure. Because the reference gradient is defined as the median of the per-pass values, it is insensitive to these extremes.
