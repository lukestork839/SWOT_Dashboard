Figure 1. Study area and the arc-based sampling frame of the digital elevation model (DEM) analysis: the Kanektok River and Uyak Creek near Quinhagak, southwestern Alaska, two adjacent distributary channels that split at a common bifurcation and flow west to the Bering Sea. Both panels cover an identical extent and are aligned pixel for pixel. (a) Esri World Imagery overlain with the field-surveyed channel centerlines (Kanektok River in red, from an acoustic Doppler current profiler longitudinal thalweg run; Uyak Creek in blue, from a boat GPS track) and with the two corridor polygons used for the polygon-based elevation analysis (light fill, coloured edge). These polygons delineate the valley swath rather than the channels themselves: they enclose 54.3 km2 and 66.8 km2 respectively, a mean width of roughly 1.1 km when divided by the length of centerline they contain, against active channels of order 30-50 m wide, and they overlap by 2.33 km2, that overlap lying entirely within 0.3 to 3.7 km of the anchor, immediately around the bifurcation, where the two floodplains have not yet separated. (b) The same extent rendered directly from the 10 m ArcticDEM as shaded relief with a hypsometric tint, so that the basemap is itself the elevation surface every subsequent figure measures. Elevations are orthometric, converted from ArcticDEM ellipsoidal heights using a constant EGM2008 geoid separation of 13.46 m for display; the arc analysis in later figures applies the separation appropriate to each arc, which varies from 13.74 m at the anchor to 13.28 m at the coast. The colour ramp is truncated to its land portion because the standard ramp renders the near-sea-level coastal plain in the blue tones conventionally read as open water. Thin grey lines are the iso-distance arcs along which all channel, bank-crest, and floodplain elevations are sampled: arcs of constant Haversine distance from the anchor, plotted every 2 km for legibility but sampled at a 0.5 km radial step from 3.0 to 34.5 km and spanning the 248-294 degree bearing sector (dashed limbs) that contains both channels with margin. Dark points mark the position at which the DEM independently located each thalweg on each arc; their close correspondence with the field centerlines over the full 34 km indicates that the arc frame tracks the real channels rather than drifting onto the floodplain. In both panels the white circle is the anchor, the distance origin at 0 km on the shared upstream channel, and the white star is the bifurcation approximately 2.5 km downriver of it. Edge ticks are labelled in geographic coordinates, the scale bar in (a) is corrected for the latitude distortion of the Web Mercator projection, north is toward the top of the map, and the inset locates the study area within Alaska as a point, the mapped extent being too small to represent as a box at that scale. The two panels differ in epoch: the ArcticDEM v4.1 mosaic blends source acquisitions spanning 2010 to 2021, whereas the Uyak Creek centerline was surveyed in 2025 and the Kanektok River centerline in 2026.
